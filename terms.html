What Desmaptic is

Desmaptic turns an iPhone or iPad into a panel of buttons for your Mac. The app works with Desmaptic Agent, a free app for Mac that you download from this website (desmaptic.com/download); the agent isn’t distributed through the App Store. The app and the agent talk directly over your local network, through an encrypted connection; a new iPhone or iPad is paired with a code that the Mac shows. If you use iCloud, your buttons sync between your iPhone and iPad through your own iCloud account.

Your buttons run on your Mac

Buttons can run shell commands and scripts, AppleScript and Shortcuts, press keys, type text, run system actions and open apps and links. Live widgets run their scripts by themselves while your iPhone or iPad is connected — the widgets on every page of the profile you’re on, including pages that are locked or not on screen. All of this runs on your Mac with your macOS user account’s permissions, so a script can do anything your account can: change or delete files, send data over the internet, make macOS ask for permissions on the agent’s behalf.

  • Your scripts. A script you write, paste, change, or accept from an AI assistant is yours. I don’t see or check it, and I’m not responsible for what it does — unless the harm comes from a defect in Desmaptic itself. Read a script before you use it, especially one from the internet.
  • Ready-made widgets. The widgets and pages of the Gallery are written by me and are part of Desmaptic: these terms treat them as Desmaptic. A widget you’ve changed is your script. Some widgets use internet services or need macOS permissions; the Privacy Policy lists them.
  • The local API. Scripts talk to the agent on your Mac with a token that the agent keeps in ~/.desmaptic-mcp-token and replaces at least every time it starts. With the token, a program can show pictures and text on your buttons, send notifications, report button errors, and read which page and buttons are on your iPhone’s or iPad’s screen and the Mac’s sound levels. It can’t run commands: a notification it sends can only, when you tap it, open an app or a web page or do a harmless system action, such as changing the volume. Programs running under your macOS account can also use the agent’s private socket: through it, a program can do what a connected AI assistant can (see below), and anything that changes your buttons or runs a new command still waits for your approval. Keep the token private, and don’t run programs you don’t trust.
  • Keep copies of scripts that matter to you. iCloud sync is not a backup: a button you delete is deleted on all your devices.

AI assistants (optional)

You can connect an AI assistant app — for example Claude, ChatGPT or Codex, or another app that supports the Model Context Protocol — to the agent. A connected assistant can read your buttons, including their scripts; see the apps on your Mac and which one is in front, the names of your Shortcuts, what your live buttons show and how their scripts are doing; restart a live widget’s script that is already on your deck; and propose new or changed buttons, pages and profiles. Each proposal waits for you on your iPhone or iPad, and nothing changes until you accept it. If you turn on “Allow AI to Run Commands” in the agent (it’s off by default), the assistant can also ask to run a one-off command on your Mac; it runs only after you allow it on your iPhone or iPad, and the assistant gets its output.

Some assistant apps, such as Claude Code and Codex, can also run commands on your Mac by themselves, without Desmaptic; that is between you and the assistant app.

An AI assistant can be wrong. What you accept or allow is your decision. Your use of an assistant is governed by its provider’s terms and privacy policy; I’m not a party to them. What an assistant can see is described in the Privacy Policy.
